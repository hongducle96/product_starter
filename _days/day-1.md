Ask ten people what a Product Manager does and you will get ten long answers. Here is the short one.

> **The one-sentence answer**
>
> A Product Manager does not make the product. A Product Manager helps a team decide <mark>what to make, for whom, and why</mark>, then checks that it really helped.

## Explained with a toy car

Imagine you are making a toy car for a three-year-old.

1. First, find out what the child actually wants to play with.
2. Think about the car: big wheels or small, which colour, which button makes it go.
3. Ask someone who is good at building to make it.
4. Let the child play with it.
5. If the child cannot find the button, change it.
6. If the child plays happily, you got it right.

That is Product, at its simplest. Every product, from a toy to a banking app, goes around the same loop:

**A user has a problem → understand it → find a solution → build it → let people use it → look at the result → improve.**

## The grown-up version

A Product Manager is responsible for turning a customer need into a product or feature that is useful for customers and valuable for the company. They are usually not the person who codes, designs or sells. They are the person who helps the whole team answer five questions:

| Question | What it means |
|---|---|
| **What?** | What are we going to make? |
| **For whom?** | Which people, exactly? |
| **Why?** | Why does this matter, to them and to us? |
| **What first?** | Out of everything we could do, what comes first? |
| **Did it work?** | When it is done, did it actually help? |

## A quick real-world example

In a clothing app, customers say: "It is hard to find things on sale." A new PM might answer straight away: "Let's add a filter." A good PM first asks where exactly people get stuck, how many people have this problem, and whether a filter would even fix it. Read the full story in the case study [Discount hunting in a clothing app]({{ '/cases/discount-hunting/' | relative_url }}).

## How to explain any job simply

Try these five questions on any role, not just Product:

1. Who does this person help?
2. What problem do those people have?
3. What does this job do to solve it?
4. Who do they work with?
5. How do you know they did a good job?

For a PM: they help customers and the business; the problem is that a need is not met well yet; they find the problem, choose what matters, shape a solution and lead the team; they work with Design, Engineering, Business, Marketing and Operations; and they know it worked when customers are better off and the business is too.

> **Key takeaway**
>
> Product is a loop, not a one-off: <mark>understand the problem</mark>, build a small answer, check if it helped, and go again.
